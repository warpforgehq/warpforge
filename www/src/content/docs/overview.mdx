Warpforge is an **agentic development environment (ADE)**: the operating layer around your coding agents. Agent conversations, project runtime, isolated worktrees, live services, configurable multi-agent workflows, a shared memory, and human review live in one place — so running several agents does not turn into managing several terminals.

It does not replace Claude Code, Codex, or OpenCode; those tools still do the coding. Warpforge gives them shared project context, parallel execution, runtime visibility, and a reviewable path from prompt to commit. Everything runs on your machine: there is no separate Warpforge account or API key, and your existing agent authentication stays with the underlying CLI.

## Getting around

The rail on the left edge is always there. From the top:

- **The Warpforge mark**, with a light beside it that pulses while the app is reconnecting to its background service. Point at it for details.
- **New task** (<kbd>⌘N</kbd>; <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>N</kbd> starts one without a project).
- **Tasks**, **Inbox**, **Smiths** and **Mission Control** (<kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>1</kbd>–<kbd>4</kbd>). A badge counts what needs you; Mission Control lights up when a task is waiting on an answer, blocked or failed.
- **Live work** — a chip for each task that is running or waiting for you (the most recent six, then a count), showing its agent and a status light. Running tasks glow; point at a chip for its title and project, click it to open the task.
- **Update**, the panel toggle and **Settings** at the bottom.

The panel beside the rail shows what you picked: your task tree for Tasks and Mission Control, the pull requests for Inbox, your teammates for Smiths. Automations sits in the rail under the modes, and Memory lives in Settings. <kbd>{"⌘\\"}</kbd> folds the panel away and brings it back; the rail stays either way, and on a narrow window it is all that shows.

Every task you open gets a tab along the top of the window, next to the window buttons, so the few you are juggling stay one click away. Closing a tab only takes it off the strip — the task keeps running and stays in your sidebar. Drag tabs to reorder them, middle-click to close one, press <kbd>⌃Tab</kbd> / <kbd>⌃⇧Tab</kbd> to cycle, and <kbd>⌘⇧T</kbd> to bring back the last one you closed.

Settings open in place: the panel lists the sections and the page fills the main area. <kbd>Esc</kbd> takes you back.
