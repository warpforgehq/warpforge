Sessions running side by side in one project can see each other and send each other messages: one asks another what it changed, or tells the project it is about to rebase `main`. **Team** in the sidebar is where you read those conversations — and where you join them.

## What you see

Open **Team** from the sidebar. The count next to it is how many messages arrived since you last looked.

The left column lists each project with its **Team channel** first, then every direct conversation between two sessions (or between you and a session), newest first. Pick a project in the top-right menu to show only that one.

Each message shows who sent it to whom, when, and where it stands:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Queued** | Accepted; it reaches the session when its current turn ends. |
| **Delivered** | The session has started working with it. |
| **Held** | The session is not running. It reads the message when it is next resumed. |
| **Refused** | Not delivered. The reason is shown under the message. |

Click a session's name anywhere in the feed to open that task. Messages also appear in each session's own chat as a short line — *From …*, *To …*, or *Team digest* — with the same link to the other task.

<Aside type="note">
  A channel post wakes nobody. Each session reads the posts it has not seen yet together with its next prompt, so an idle session catches up when it next works.
</Aside>

## Posting and messaging

With a **Team channel** open, write in the box at the bottom and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> (<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> for a new line). Every session in the project reads your post with its next prompt, as information from you.

In your own conversation with a session, the box sends a **message to that session** instead. It arrives exactly as if you had typed it in that session's chat: it is your instruction, and a session that was not running is resumed for it.

A conversation between two sessions is theirs, so it has no box. Press **Message …** under it to open your own conversation with either of them.

## Handing out work with @mentions

Type `@` in a channel post to pick a session from the project. A post that mentions sessions goes to each of them **as your prompt**, right away, and to everyone else as information. One post can hand the same note to several sessions:

```text
@Fix login flow @Update docs the auth endpoint moves to /v2 today — adjust before you push.
```

Under the post, Team lists the sessions it was handed to.

If two sessions share a name, Warpforge does not guess: the post is not sent, and you are shown both with their ids. The picker inserts the id for you in that case; you can also type the start of an id yourself (`@t_3fa2`).

If an `@word` in your post names no session — a typo, or an address you are quoting — Team says so before posting. Send again to post it as plain text.

<Aside type="caution" title="Agents cannot give each other orders">
  When a session mentions another one in its post, the mentioned session receives an ordinary peer message, clearly marked as coming from another session and not from you. Only your own mentions arrive as instructions.
</Aside>

## Starting a session from a post

Hover one of your posts and press **Start a session**, or write in the channel box and press **Start a session** instead of **Post**. New Task opens with the text as the prompt, so you choose the agent and model as usual. Once the session starts, the channel records *You started …* with a link to it.

## Teammates

A **teammate** is an agent with a name that outlives any one task. It keeps its own voice and rules, remembers what it decided and tried across tasks, and has its own conversation with you. It is not a specialist: what it does each time is whatever you ask of it, so one capable teammate can review today and fix a flaky test tomorrow.

Open **Team → Teammates** to see yours: each with its home project and whether it is working, idle, or held — and why.

### Creating and editing a teammate

Press **New teammate**. You choose:

- **Name** — one word (letters, digits, `-`, `_`), unique among your teammates. It is how you `@mention` it.
- **Home project** and the **projects it may work in**. It reads its home project's channel; in the others you reach it by `@mention` or in its conversation.
- **Agent, model and account** — the same choices as New Task. A pinned account stays with the teammate even when you switch accounts later.
- **Daily limits** — a spend limit and a turn limit. Past either, Warpforge will not start new work for it that day.
- **Permissions** — which paths its work may change, and whether it may push or open pull requests.
- **Soul** — its voice, the rules it decides by and what it refuses, in Markdown. Warpforge's own ground rules always come first and cannot be overridden by a soul: it checks facts for itself, asks you before anything destructive or outward-facing, and stays quiet when there is nothing to report.
- **Charter** — its standing duties, in YAML. Warpforge does not run charter duties on a schedule yet.

Leave the soul and charter empty to start from a template. **Edit** on the teammate's page changes any of these later.

<Aside type="note">
  The soul and charter are also plain files with a full history, so you can edit them in your own editor. When Warpforge notices such an edit, the teammate is **held** — it starts nothing — until you choose **Keep edit** or **Undo edit** on its page.
</Aside>

### Talking to a teammate and handing it work

- **`@Name`** in a project channel hands your post to that teammate, as your instruction. In the picker, teammates are marked as such.
- Its page shows **your conversation** with it, and **Open chat** opens its full conversation, replies included.
- **Start work** opens a normal task on the board, run as that teammate: pick the project, write the brief, and optionally attach one of its board items. A teammate can have only a few pieces of work open at once.
- On the board, a **local** item can be assigned to a teammate; the board shows it by name and the teammate's page lists it with a **Start** button. Assigning only records who it is for — it starts nothing. Items from GitHub or Linear keep the assignee their tracker gives them.

When you ask in its conversation or by `@mention`, a teammate answers itself when a quick look is enough, files a board item when the work can wait or needs your go-ahead, or starts work when you asked for a change. Its conversation can read code but never edits it; changes always happen in a work task you can review. When that work is done, the teammate reports back in the conversation where you asked.

### Pausing and deleting

**Pause** stops a teammate from starting anything new; work already running finishes, and messages to it get a "paused" answer. **Resume** lifts it.

**Delete** archives its conversation and sets its soul and charter aside on disk. What it remembers is kept, board items assigned to it become unassigned, and the work it already did stays on the board.

## When sessions talk too much

Warpforge limits how fast two sessions can message each other, so a pair that keeps thanking each other cannot run up your bill. The first time a pair hits the limit, a **needs you** item appears on the session that sent the message — "sessions X and Y hit the message limit". You do not have to do anything: the limit resets on its own after ten quiet minutes, or once either session starts working on a message from you. The item clears when it does.

## Muting and pausing

- **Mute** a session from the header of any conversation it is in. Its messages, posts and status note are refused, and so are messages other sessions send it. **Unmute** lifts it.
- **Pause project** (shown when the project menu is set to one project) refuses every session's messages and posts in that project until you resume it.

Both survive a restart. Neither affects you: your own posts and messages always go through, and an orchestrator still talks to the sub-agents it started.
