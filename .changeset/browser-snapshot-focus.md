Agents now browse the built-in browser more than twice as efficiently. In our benchmark of everyday tasks — filling forms, working through dialogs, reading long articles, paging through endless lists — agents read 57% less page text and needed a third fewer steps, which means lower token bills and faster results. On long pages the savings are bigger still: reading one section of a Wikipedia article now costs about 85% less.

Agents can map a long page by its headings and jump straight to the section they need, see a form's result or a dialog's contents right after they click, press keys like Escape and Tab, scroll long pages and lazy-loading lists, and wait for something to appear instead of checking again and again. They no longer fall back to screenshots to read lists — in the benchmark they needed none at all.
